# Sound Island Adventure

The web companion to a cross-curricular course built on the Voyager Golden Record. Sixth-graders are commissioned as cultural envoys, and their task is to cross thirteen soundscape stations, collect the sounds that represent their school, and press a golden record to send out into the world.

Sound Island Adventure is the featured case study of **Dato Music Lab** (https://datomusiclab.dpdns.org), a working elementary music teacher's studio in Taipei.

## What it is

In 1977 two Voyager probes left Earth carrying a gold-plated record of what this planet sounds like. That is an extraordinary premise for a school project, because it forces a real question: out of everything you could record, what actually represents the place you live — and how would you defend that choice to someone who has never been here?

The course puts that question to sixth-graders in their final year of primary school. This page is the map they move through: thirteen stations, unlocked one at a time, each one a listening task somewhere in the school or the surrounding neighbourhood. The reward for finishing is not a score. It is a completed record and a sentence the students can now defend — *this is why this is our place*.

The commission has two real deliverables behind it: a physical interactive record exhibition shared between four schools, and a soundscape exchange with a partner school overseas.

## Features

**Thirteen stations, unlocked in sequence.** Each glowing point on the map opens a level; finishing it unlocks the next and earns a fragment of the golden record.

**A guide who asks the right question.** A soundscape navigator prompts students to listen for what the sound reveals about the place, rather than simply naming it.

**Structured for a 40-minute lesson.** Every station carries its mission flow, its clear condition, and the deliverable the curator expects.

**A teacher console.** Unlock the whole map for a projected lesson, force-complete the current level, or clear local progress before the next class.

**Progress saved locally.** Everything persists in the browser, so a class can stop mid-voyage and pick it up next week.

## How to use

Download the folder and open `index.html`. Best on a computer with a projector; press `F` for fullscreen.

Students should be wearing headphones and carrying something to record with — the page is the map and the brief, but the actual work happens out in the corridors, the playground and the streets around the school.

## Tech

A single HTML file with no dependencies, no build step and no network access. Progress lives in the browser's local storage.
